Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is increasingly deployed to support developer querying over complex technical documentation. 
However, standard pipelines predominantly rely on fixed-size sliding window chunking, which disregards the rich hierarchical syntax inherent to Markdown sources.
In this paper, we present a controlled empirical evaluation comparing three distinct chunking strategies on technical documentation: (i)~conventional Fixed-size chunking (512 tokens, 64-token overlap), (ii)~Structure-aware chunking guided by Markdown heading hierarchy and atomic block preservation, and (iii)~Prompt-based chunking leveraging LLMs for block boundary proposal with deterministic local validation.
We evaluate these strategies on a dedicated benchmark constructed from the official Angular documentation, comprising 384 technical documents, 140 expert-curated question-answering pairs across three difficulty tiers (60 easy, 40 medium, 40 hard), and 233 granular evidence spans with no ground-truth coupling to chunk IDs.
Within a controlled evaluation pipeline using \texttt{text-embedding-3-small}, cosine similarity, and fixed retrieval/context token budgets (2,048 and 4,096 tokens), our results reveal nuanced trade-offs across three research questions: 
Fixed-size chunking achieves the highest early retrieval ranking (Hit@1 and MRR) and overall answer Token F1; 
Structure-aware chunking dominates in broad evidence recovery (Hit@10, Recall@10, Evidence Coverage, and All-Evidence-Retrieved Rate); 
and Prompt-based chunking achieves the highest Token Recall but fails to demonstrate an overall performance advantage while incurring high latency and token overhead. 
Our findings demonstrate that no single chunking strategy is universally optimal for all RAG objectives, highlighting the necessity of tailoring chunking paradigms to specific application trade-offs.
