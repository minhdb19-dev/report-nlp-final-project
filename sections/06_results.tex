\subsection{RQ1: Early Retrieval Ranking vs. Deep Candidate Recall}
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, the retrieval ranking results demonstrate a striking divergence between top-1 precision and top-10 recall:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fixed-Size Dominance in Early Ranking:} Fixed-size chunking secures the highest Hit@1 ($0.671$) and MRR ($0.742$). The larger, uniform 512-token chunks with 64-token overlap concentrate multiple co-occurring query keywords into a single dense embedding, yielding strong top-1 similarity scores.
    \item \textbf{Structure-Aware Superiority at Deeper Ranks:} In contrast, Structure-aware chunking achieves the highest Hit@10 ($0.921$) and Recall@10 ($0.893$). Because structure-aware chunks respect section boundaries and do not coalesce sibling sections, they avoid semantic dilution and successfully elevate relevant sub-sections into the top-10 candidate pool.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ2: Evidence Coverage and All-Evidence Recovery}
In multi-span evidence grounding, Structure-aware chunking demonstrates decisive empirical superiority:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High Evidence Coverage:} Structure-aware chunking recovers $86.3\%$ of all gold evidence spans, outperforming Fixed-size ($71.4\%$) by $+14.9\%$ absolute.
    \item \textbf{All-Evidence-Retrieved Rate (AER):} For complex queries requiring multiple evidence spans, Structure-aware chunking achieves an AER of $71.4\%$ (vs.\ $52.8\%$ for Fixed-size). Because AST chunks do not bisect code listings, complete functional evidence is preserved intact.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ3: Answer Generation and Lexical Fidelity}
Downstream answer synthesis under \texttt{gpt-5-mini} reveals a notable trade-off between precision and recall:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fixed-Size Answer F1:} Fixed-size chunking attains the highest overall Token F1 ($62.5$) and Token Precision ($63.8$). The fewer, larger chunks present a less fragmented reading context to the generator, reducing synthesis noise.
    \item \textbf{Prompt-Based Token Recall:} Prompt-based chunking achieves the highest Token Recall ($66.8$), as the model-guided segments capture rich peripheral context, though sometimes at the cost of precision.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance Stratification by Difficulty Tier}
Table~\ref{tab:difficulty_breakdown} details how chunking strategies perform across query difficulty tiers (Easy: 60, Medium: 40, Hard: 40). On Easy single-section queries, Fixed-size chunking performs best; however, on Hard cross-document queries requiring multi-span aggregation, Structure-aware chunking delivers the highest AER and answer completeness.
